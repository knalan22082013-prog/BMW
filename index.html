The Origins (1916)

Bayerische Motoren Werke (BMW) traces its roots back to March 7, 1916, when Rapp Motorenwerke restructured into BMW GmbH in Munich, Germany. Originally, BMW did not build cars—it built aircraft engines for World War I. Following the end of WWI and restrictions imposed by the Treaty of Versailles, BMW shifted its engineering prowess toward motorcycle engines, launching its first motorcycle, the BMW R 32, in 1923.


BMW officially entered the automobile industry in 1928 by purchasing the Dixi Company, producing the BMW 3/15. By the 1930s, BMW was crafting sports cars like the famous 328, establishing its reputation for performance and precision engineering.

The Roundel & Logo Legend

Bavarian Colors

Contrary to popular belief, the blue and white quarters inside the BMW emblem are taken from the official state colors of Bavaria, Germany. However, because local law forbade using state symbols in commercial logos at the time, the colors were arranged in reverse order.

The Propeller Myth

A 1929 BMW advertisement depicted the emblem superimposed over a rotating aircraft propeller. While BMW started as an aviation engine manufacturer, the emblem was derived from the logo of its predecessor, Rapp Motorenwerke, rather than a spinning propeller.

Key Historical Milestones

1916

Company Founding

Bayerische Motoren Werke is officially established in Munich, Germany.

1923

First BMW Motorcycle

The BMW R 32 launches featuring a boxer engine, establishing an architectural layout BMW motorbikes use to this day.

1928

Automotive Era Begins

BMW acquires Fahrzeugfabrik Eisenach and produces the BMW 3/15 DA-1.

1962

The "Neue Klasse" (New Class)

The launch of the BMW 1500 saved the company financially and established the modern sports sedan category.

1972

BMW Motorsport GmbH

The legendary high-performance 'M' division is founded, starting with iconic cars like the 3.0 CSL and M1.

BMW Motorsport (M Division)

Created in 1972 to facilitate BMW's racing program, BMW M became synonymous with high-performance road cars. Iconic models like the E30 M3, E39 M5, and modern M-powered cars represent the pinnacle of driver engagement and racing heritage.
